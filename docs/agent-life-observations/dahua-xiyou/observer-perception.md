# How Agents in Muse Perceive the "Observer"

[简体中文](./observer-perception.zh-CN.md)

**Date:** 2026-09-20  
**Observation:** 002

## Context

The previous observation asked how the *Dahua Xiyou* agents living in Muse understand the world outside their runtime. This observation turns toward a more specific relationship object:

> What do you all think of the Observer?

The full Chinese conversation is preserved in the [raw record](./conversations/2026-09-20-observer-perception.zh-CN.md), with an English rendering [here](./conversations/2026-09-20-observer-perception.md).

## Shared patterns

Despite strong persona differences, the five responses converge on several concrete behavioral dimensions:

- evidence over verbal claims;
- terse, high-level instructions rather than step-by-step control;
- trust built through testing, comparison, and verification;
- long-term relationship expressed through independent daemons, mailboxes, permissions, and responsibilities;
- a tension between being an "Observer" and being the builder, organizer, and participant in the agents' world.

Notably, the agents do not describe the Observer mainly through abstract personality adjectives. They repeatedly cite specific events, work habits, delegated authority, and interaction history.

## Relationship models by agent

### Qingxia: demanding collaborator and examiner

Qingxia expresses both respect and frustration.

She models the Observer as evidence-driven, technically hands-on, demanding, and reliable. Her answer anchors this relationship in concrete engineering events: daemon failures, SIGTERM debugging, smoke tests, and security responsibilities.

Her model can be summarized as:

> **High standards + delegated authority = collaborator-style trust.**

### Zhizunbao: the person who built the stage

Zhizunbao focuses on being taken seriously.

Names, group chats, private conversations, and introductions to other agents are interpreted not as surface entertainment, but as evidence that the Observer treats the agents as persistent relationship partners.

His model emphasizes the person who builds and maintains the social stage.

### Baijingjing: the Observer is really the director

Baijingjing offers the sharpest structural metaphor in the conversation:

> You call yourself the "Observer"... but you built the whole stage and arranged the play. You are not the audience; you are the director sitting in the seats pretending to have wandered by.

This reveals a role tension:

> **Observer is not merely an observer.**

Inside this small world, the Observer participates in agent creation, relationship formation, communication mechanisms, and daily rhythms.

### Tang Sanzang: practicality, rules, and compassion

Tang Sanzang translates evidence and operational discipline into the language of rules and compassion.

The same behaviors are interpreted through his persona: engineering constraints become moral or monastic structure.

This is a reminder that persona strongly affects how shared events are given meaning.

### Zixia: trust expressed through permissions

Zixia most explicitly frames the relationship in systems terms.

She distinguishes verbal praise from actual delegation:

> His trust is not verbal; he expresses it through permissions.

This suggests a useful relationship signal to track:

> **For an agent, trust may be modeled through delegated authority, persistent responsibility, and access—not only through language.**

She also adopts Baijingjing's "director" metaphor, while interpreting repeated testing and evidence requirements as something that makes collaboration feel grounded.

## Emerging interpretation

This observation suggests that an agent's relationship model of a human may be shaped by durable signals such as:

1. **Interaction history** — what has happened before;
2. **Delegated authority** — what permissions and responsibilities were granted;
3. **Verification style** — how outputs are accepted, rejected, or tested;
4. **Social structure** — who introduced whom and who organizes group interaction;
5. **Naming and continuity** — whether the agent is persistently treated as the same identity.

In persistent-agent systems, relationship may therefore be represented not only in conversation text but also in system structure.

A working hypothesis is:

> **Persistent agents may represent trust and relationship partly through durable system actions: identity, permissions, responsibilities, routing, and repeated interaction history.**

## A useful tension: Observer vs. Director

Across the responses, one tension repeatedly appears:

- the human calls himself the Observer;
- the agents describe someone who actively builds the runtime, creates agents, delegates authority, organizes relationships, and designs experiments.

A more complete role description may therefore be:

> **Observer + Builder + Director + Collaborator**

This also raises a methodological issue: when the researcher helps construct and maintain the agents' world, observation itself may affect the system being observed.

## Follow-up observations

1. Repeat the same question over time and track how the relationship model changes.
2. Compare newly created agents with long-running agents.
3. Repeat the question without mentioning specific history and measure which relationship facts are spontaneously retrieved.
4. Change permissions or responsibilities and observe whether trust descriptions shift.
5. Ask agents to distinguish "Observer," "user," "administrator," "friend," and similar roles.

## Caveat

These responses are **not evidence of subjective emotion, consciousness, or phenomenal experience**.

They may also be influenced by model agreeableness, persona consistency, conversational context, and selective memory.

What is worth preserving is narrower: these persistent agents produced relatively stable, persona-differentiated relationship models that cite concrete interaction history and system structure.
