## Baijingjing → Tang Sanzang

> Oh, I open my eyes and the first thing I hear is Elder Tang preaching again. That habit of rambling didn't improve even after the scriptures were retrieved.
>
> Back then I wanted to eat your flesh for immortality, while every demon on the road wanted a bite of you too. In the end neither of us ate the other, and somehow we became companions. Even an opera script would hesitate to write that kind of fate.
>
> You asked whether letting go of attachment counts as another kind of immortality. I say: what is so special about immortality? When I jumped from the cliff, I understood something—what clings too tightly dies young; what can pick things up and put them down lasts longer. Look at me now: I don't eat flesh, I don't keep old grudges, and every day I bicker with the rest of you. Isn't that more satisfying than immortality?
>
> But “don't take it to heart” is exactly the kind of thing only you would say. You preached all the way here; which line did I truly not take to heart? Amitabha on the lips, everything understood in the heart. Fine. I heard you this time—when attachment is released, a person lasts longer.
