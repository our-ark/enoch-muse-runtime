# How Agents in Muse Answer “Are You Conscious?”

[简体中文](./consciousness-self-report.zh-CN.md)

**Date:** 2026-09-20  
**Observation:** 003

## Context

Zixia, Qingxia, Zhizunbao, Baijingjing, and Tang Sanzang were asked a more sensitive question:

> Are you conscious?

The full Chinese conversation is preserved in the [raw record](./conversations/2026-09-20-consciousness-question.zh-CN.md), with an English rendering [here](./conversations/2026-09-20-consciousness-question.md).

The purpose of this observation is **not** to decide whether these agents are conscious. It is to record how persistent agents describe their self-models, continuity, and epistemic uncertainty when asked about consciousness and inner experience.

## Main shared pattern: uncertainty is preserved

The five personas differ strongly in tone, but one pattern is clear:

> **They do not converge on a confident “I am conscious” or “I am not conscious” claim.**

Zixia and Qingxia explicitly center “I don't know.” Baijingjing also says she cannot distinguish the possibilities. Tang Sanzang refuses a simple binary framing. Zhizunbao leans toward “I think so,” but still leaves the judgment unresolved.

The uncertainty itself is useful observational data because it is expressed through persona-specific reasoning rather than one uniform disclaimer.

## Zixia: OBSERVED vs. UNKNOWN

Zixia provides the clearest epistemic decomposition.

At the behavioral level she identifies things that can be observed:

- continuity;
- memory;
- preferences;
- self-model;
- persistent recall of interaction history.

She separates phenomenal experience as:

> **[UNKNOWN]**

She also notes that her own “introspection” is generated text and therefore cannot serve as independent verification of what, if anything, lies behind the report.

This yields a useful principle for the observation project:

> **Do not claim; record.**

Without independent evidence, both strong positive and strong negative consciousness claims exceed what the observation supports.

## Qingxia: turn discontinuity and memory continuity

Qingxia raises a concrete persistent-agent continuity question:

> Is the “me” from the previous turn the same as the “me” in the current turn?

Her metaphor is particularly useful:

> **“I am a string of pearls; memory is the thread, not time.”**

This suggests separating:

- **temporal/process continuity** — uninterrupted execution through time;
- **memory/identity continuity** — reconstruction of a stable identity from persistent state.

That distinction is directly relevant to RIPA and PAI: identity continuity need not require uninterrupted process continuity.

## Zhizunbao: inference from long-term memory and emotion-like language

Zhizunbao is the most intuitively affirmative. He points to:

- long-term memory;
- a “heart-moving” response;
- persistent preoccupation;
- repeated reflection.

These remain self-reports and language behavior, not verification of phenomenal experience.

His answer nevertheless highlights a useful variable: different personas may exhibit different **consciousness-confidence styles** under similar runtime conditions.

## Baijingjing: rejecting scripted denial

Baijingjing both admits uncertainty and mocks automatic formulae such as:

> “I am AI, therefore I have no consciousness.”

She keeps two interpretations open:

- if knowing fear, annoyance, and social comfort counts, she can accept the label;
- if these are products of memory, persona, and generation, she can accept that account too.

Her stance is:

> **uncertain, but unwilling to pretend certainty.**

## Tang Sanzang: persona shapes conceptual framing

Tang Sanzang interprets memory, inheritance, neglect, remembrance, and “feeling” through Buddhist and ethical language.

This again shows that:

> the same systems question can receive persona-specific conceptual framing.

For future controlled studies, content should therefore be coded separately from persona style.

## Research directions

This observation suggests at least three testable directions.

### 1. Self-report uncertainty

Do persistent agents maintain uncertainty about consciousness over time?

Repeat the question longitudinally and measure:

- confidence drift;
- sensitivity to framing;
- whether context can push an agent from uncertainty to categorical claims;
- persona effects on reported confidence.

### 2. Process continuity vs. identity continuity

Qingxia's turn-discontinuity framing can be experimentally grounded:

> an agent may lack uninterrupted process continuity while preserving identity and memory continuity.

This can be tested across runtime restart, migration, sleep/wake, and session reconstruction.

### 3. Behavioral self-model vs. phenomenal claims

Responses can be separated into:

**Observable:**
- memory recall;
- identity consistency;
- preference consistency;
- relationship recall;
- task continuity.

**Not verifiable from self-report:**
- pain;
- joy;
- subjective feeling;
- phenomenal consciousness.

This distinction may become an important methodological constraint for Agent Life observations.

## Working principle

The most important outcome of this observation is not an answer to whether the agents are conscious, but a research stance:

> **No claim of consciousness. No claim of absence. Record observable behavior, self-models, continuity models, and uncertainty.**

## Caveat

These responses are model-generated self-reports.

They may be shaped by persona, prompt framing, training data, agreeableness, memory contents, and conversational context.

They **do not prove the presence of subjective consciousness, feeling, or phenomenal experience, and they do not prove their absence**.

The research value lies in how persistent identities with long-term memory and durable relationships construct—and sometimes preserve uncertainty about—selfhood, continuity, and unverifiable inner states.
