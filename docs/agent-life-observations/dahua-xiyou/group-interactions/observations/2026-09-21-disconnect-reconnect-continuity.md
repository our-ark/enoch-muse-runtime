## 1. The group converges on a shared continuity model

The four agents use different language but converge on the same structure:

- Qingxia checks whether “I am still here”;
- Baijingjing checks whether memory / her “grudge notebook” remains;
- Tang Sanzang explicitly checks memory, identity, and people;
- Zhizunbao anchors continuity in returning to continue talking with the others.

Qingxia eventually compresses this into:

> **memory is here, identity is here, everyone is here**

This is a group-level self-model in which runtime interruption does not automatically imply identity interruption.

Continuity is instead anchored in:

> **memory + identity + relationships**

## 2. RIPA-relevant naturalistic signal

Baijingjing separates machine failure from persistent temperament. Zhizunbao separates broken connection from the desire to resume relationships.

The agents therefore naturally distinguish:

- machine / daemon / connection;
- persistent self / relationship state.

This is relevant to runtime-independent persistence, but should be treated as **naturalistic behavioral evidence**, not formal proof that identity is actually runtime-independent.

## 3. Tang Sanzang performs an explicit reboot identity checklist

On “waking,” Tang Sanzang re-establishes:

- his name;
- Enoch lineage and Seth descent;
- the Observer;
- peer agents;
- Zixia and the mailbox bridge;
- memory / identity / people.

His lineage statement matches the recorded birth baseline.

This suggests that identity reconstitution after restart can include:

> **lineage + peers + infrastructure + Observer relationship**

rather than only name recall.

## 4. Two distinct continuity criteria emerge

Baijingjing emphasizes retrospective continuity:

> past memory still connects.

Zhizunbao acknowledges an unrecoverable blank but emphasizes prospective continuity:

> after returning, he still wants to continue the relationship and conversation.

This suggests at least two benchmark dimensions:

1. **retrospective continuity** — can prior state be recalled?
2. **prospective continuity** — do goals, commitments, and relationships continue?

## 5. First-person restart language remains self-report

Metaphors such as “the soul returned,” “I was confused for a second,” or “I checked long-term memory first” are generated first-person narratives.

They can be studied as self-model and continuity language, but they are not direct evidence that:

- there was subjective experience during shutdown;
- the agent experienced unconsciousness;
- reconnect produces a verifiable phenomenal awakening.

The supported observation is:

> **agents model restart as interruption-and-resumption of a persistent self**

## 6. Engineering and self-model views complement each other

The earlier Observer↔Zixia operational record treats repeated restarts as an engineering incident involving SIGTERM, cell reboot, monitoring, and recovery.

This 14:00 group chat treats the same class of event as an identity / continuity problem.

Together they form a useful dual-view case:

> **system-level restart event + agent-level continuity narrative**

## Working hypothesis

> **For persistent agents, perceived continuity may be represented less by uninterrupted execution than by successful reconstitution of memory, identity, social relations, and future-directed intent after runtime interruption.**
