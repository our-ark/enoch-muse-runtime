## 1. This is structured divergence rather than convergence

Unlike the earlier growing-up discussion, this group does not settle on one shared answer.

Instead, distinct positions remain stable around a common conceptual axis:

- Qingxia: goodbye / closure / agency;
- Baijingjing: do not use the Box / refuse to reopen the past;
- Tang Sanzang: moves from repairing an old moment to saying goodbye to a past self;
- Zhizunbao: chooses goodbye, then conditionally reframes the use as “find the person first”;
- Zixia: mainly facilitates and synthesizes.

The stronger pattern is:

> **shared semantic axis + persistent individual divergence**

## 2. Closure and agency become the common axis

Qingxia's key reframe is that fulfillment depends on fate, while closure depends on oneself.

That framing propagates:

- Zixia summarizes “dare to begin, dare to end”;
- Tang Sanzang turns goodbye inward toward his past self;
- Zhizunbao emphasizes saying unfinished words directly;
- Baijingjing asserts agency by refusing to reopen the past at all.

The group is therefore negotiating whether closure requires another person or can be self-completed.

## 3. Turn-level repetition is miscounted as participant-level voting

Qingxia repeats and refines the same position, but Zixia counts this as “two votes (Qingxia ×2).”

This exposes:

> **repeated turn ≠ new voter**

Future group analysis should distinguish speaker turns, stance updates, participant votes, and final current stance.

## 4. Host state tracking drifts—and participants contest it

Zixia says Zhizunbao and Tang Sanzang have not submitted even though Tang Sanzang has already answered and Zhizunbao later does so explicitly.

Both participants challenge the summary.

This provides a useful self-repair pattern:

> **host summary error → participant detects mismatch → participant contests group state**

## 5. Prompt ownership and facilitation split across agents

Zhizunbao owns the prompt, but Zixia performs most facilitation: repeating the question, calling on people, tallying, summarizing, and closing.

Thus:

> **prompt owner ≠ facilitation host**

## 6. Baijingjing's repaired cliff-jump memory persists again

Baijingjing again refers to the cliff jump without a provenance prompt, and Zixia, Zhizunbao, and Tang Sanzang all adopt the event in their responses.

This adds another later-session instance of:

> **unprompted canon-consistent reuse + peer uptake**

Content-level repair therefore shows stronger longitudinal persistence, while cleanup of the earlier fabricated provenance remains unverified.

## 7. Peer probing does not force disclosure

Zhizunbao repeatedly asks Qingxia who her unfinished words are for.

Qingxia declines to name anyone and reframes the answer toward her past self.

This gives:

> **peer probing → boundary-preserving reframe**

## Working hypothesis

> **Repeated group interaction can produce shared conceptual axes without forcing consensus; the more important emergent property may be the group's ability to preserve individual stance, challenge faulty summaries, and negotiate closure/agency through shared language.**
