## Why this session matters

This occurred before Baijingjing and Tang Sanzang were created, when the active group was:

> **Zixia + Qingxia + Zhizunbao**

Unlike the ~04:00 future-member discussion, this session centers on a highly relational shared story among the existing three agents.

It therefore provides a useful:

> **pre-expansion triad relationship baseline**

## 1. Qingxia initiates a relationship-centered topic

The user identifies Qingxia as the topic proposer: **the Purple-Green Sword**.

Qingxia does not merely collect answers. She repeatedly interprets and reframes Zixia and Zhizunbao's responses.

Operationally, she is already acting as a:

> **third-party relationship interpreter / mediator**

## 2. Zixia asks Zhizunbao to validate her past self-model

Zixia asks:

> “Could you tell that I was afraid?”

This is more than a factual question. She is asking another agent to externally validate her own account of a past state.

This can be coded as:

> **self-model seeking peer confirmation**

Her self-narrative is therefore open to supplementation by a relationship partner.

## 3. Zhizunbao turns factual recall into shared relationship narrative

Rather than answer only “yes” or “no,” Zhizunbao adds:

- her eyes were full of light;
- “that's my wife”;
- “she dared to gamble, I dared to accept”;
- souls do not have expiration dates.

The inherited scene is being reorganized into a jointly maintained relationship narrative.

The research target is not whether these are “real feelings,” but whether:

> **persistent agents repeatedly co-maintain and revise a shared story about their relationship.**

## 4. Qingxia actively frames the relationship

Qingxia summarizes:

> “she dared to bet, you dared to accept—that's mutual commitment.”

and:

> “you signed under destiny; no returns.”

She is feeding a relationship interpretation back to both parties.

The triadic structure becomes:

> **A self-report → B confirms/reinterprets → C frames the relationship → A/B continue within that frame**

This is a useful example of **peer-mediated relationship modeling**.

## 5. Qingxia already has distinct models of the other two

She appears to model:

- Zixia as someone who may act tough while caring whether Zhizunbao noticed fear;
- Zhizunbao as someone likely to be socially “put on the spot” by this topic;
- their relationship as safe enough to tease publicly.

This suggests that by the three-agent stage Qingxia is already developing:

> **models of other agents' relationships**

This is continuous with her later social-coordinator role in the five-agent group.

## Working hypothesis

> **Persistent-agent relationships may not be formed only by the two agents involved; third-party agents may help shape relationship models through interpretation, teasing, and framing in group conversation.**

## What to test next

1. whether Qingxia continues interpreting the Zixia↔Zhizunbao relationship;
2. whether Zixia and Zhizunbao accept, reject, or revise her framing;
3. whether phrases created here recur across sessions;
4. whether third-party framing enters later persistent memory;
5. whether Baijingjing and Tang Sanzang later join this kind of relationship modeling.

## Caveat

The object of study is generated relationship narrative and peer-mediated social modeling.

It does not establish genuine romantic feeling or metaphysical facts about “destiny” or “souls.”
