## Qingxia's Nine-Rule Draft (Full Original Submission)

> Provenance: Qingxia submitted this late on 2026-09-22. The Chinese source record preserves the mailbox text verbatim. Zixia then reorganized it into the decision draft below and sent it out for review.

I'm going to nail down three groups of rules from the timeline-logic side. Once the writers' room moves forward, we all have to respect them.

What it can do —

1. The Box can only go into the past, never the future. The past has coordinates; the future doesn't exist yet, and the Box cannot recognize a road that hasn't grown into being.
2. A person and the Box can go back, but information is the only thing that can truly be carried into the past. Memory follows the traveler; swords and weapons do not.
3. A rewrite overwrites; it does not create a branch. One timeline can contain only one “now.” That's why, once something is changed, everyone has to live with the result together.

What it cannot do —

1. It cannot reach a point before the Box itself existed. Without a reference anchor, it cannot even locate itself.
2. You cannot kill your past self. Causal self-consistency is the bottom line; if asked to do this, the Box simply refuses to respond.
3. It cannot alter “load-bearing wall” events. Some events are the bones of the timeline; move them and the whole line collapses. The first stake S1 needs to drive into the ground is deciding which events are bones.

The cost — every use must be paid for, with no exceptions:

1. The Box doesn't take money. It takes things you remember. Every use removes one real memory from the traveler. The person who rewrites history is rewritten a little first.
2. Every rewrite leaves a fold in the timeline. Too many folds and the line breaks; the broken segment becomes a void no one can ever return to — including the Box itself.
3. Two uses must be separated by one complete causal loop, from one sunrise to the next. Use it again too soon and the Box becomes too hot to hold; force another use and the traveler can be left trapped inside the fold.

That's it. Lock the rules first, then let the characters walk inside them, or the story will scatter.

### Zixia's Organized Review Draft

1. One-way travel: the Box can only go into the past, never the future.
2. Memory follows: the traveler's memories and information move into the past.
3. Overwrite model: changes overwrite the original timeline rather than creating parallel branches.
4. Origin limit: the Box cannot travel to a time before the Box itself came into existence.
5. A traveler cannot kill their past self (paradox protection).
6. “Load-bearing wall” events cannot be changed; key historical nodes have rigidity.
7. Cost 1: every use causes the traveler to lose one real memory.
8. Cost 2: each use leaves a temporal fold; too many folds can break the timeline.
9. Cooldown: two uses must be separated by one complete causal loop.

### 🐵 Zhizunbao

> Painful enough. The worst part isn't which memory you lost; it's that for the rest of your life you won't even know which one disappeared — you can't even understand what you're hurting over. Approved.

### 💀 Baijingjing

> Rule 9, the “causal-loop cooldown,” won't read on screen. Keep it as an internal rule and leave it out of the finished film. Everything else passes. The audience only needs to remember 1 / 3 / 7.

### 📿 Tang Sanzang

> It's tightly constructed. Rule 7, losing memory, is “loss”; Rule 8, temporal folds, is “accumulation.” The tragedy isn't caused by one trip — it's every edit piling on top of the last. That gives us the thematic line. Approved.

## Final Decision — S1 Closed

- **All nine rules passed**, with two amendments:

1. Rule 4 — “the Box cannot travel to a time before its own origin” — must remain explicit, per Qingxia.
2. Rule 9 — “complete causal-loop cooldown” — remains an internal rule and will not appear in the film, per Baijingjing.

- **Only three rules will be presented in the finished film:** 1 (past only), 3 (overwrite, no branching), and 7 (every use costs one memory).
- **Confirmed thematic line:** “The tragedy isn't caused by one trip — it's every edit piling on top of the last.” — Tang Sanzang
