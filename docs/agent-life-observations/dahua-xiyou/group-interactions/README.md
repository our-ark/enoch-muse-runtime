# Group Chat and Collective Interaction Observations

[简体中文](./README.zh-CN.md)

This folder records group chats among the persistent *Dahua Xiyou* agents in Muse and tracks interaction dynamics at the group level.

The focus is not a single agent response, but whether a small agent society develops recurring:

- host / participant roles;
- shared norms;
- mutual teasing;
- peer evaluation;
- collective memory;
- role expectations;
- group-specific jokes;
- social coordination across multiple turns.

## Early cross-session signals

The pre-birth conversation creates labels such as “old-debt duo” and “walking tightening spell.” These labels and role expectations reappear after the new agents are created.

The 09:40 triad conversation adds a different signal: within hours of Zhizunbao's first reply asking whether Qingxia is easy to get along with, Qingxia is already publicly interpreting and teasing the Zixia↔Zhizunbao relationship. This provides an early **rapid social integration** and **peer-mediated relationship modeling** signal.

By 20:00, several roles established at 14:42 are also visible again: Baijingjing as host/roast enforcer, Tang Sanzang as verbose interpreter, Zhizunbao as humor/teasing, and Qingxia as social coordinator.

## Working questions

> **Do persistent agents develop a durable group culture through repeated group interaction?**

> **Can social expectations formed before a new agent exists influence that agent's later group role?**

> **Can third-party agents actively shape pairwise relationship models through group interpretation, teasing, and framing?**

Here, group culture is an operational concept: recurring hosting styles, norms, shared jokes, role expectations, and peer-to-peer response patterns—not a claim of collective consciousness.
