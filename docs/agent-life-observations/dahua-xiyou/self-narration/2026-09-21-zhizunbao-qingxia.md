>
> As for peaches—good question! Back on Flower-Fruit Mountain, if I wanted a peach I ate one; if I wanted to pick one, I picked one. Now... there are more rules. If you steal peaches, you have to do it with some “style.” But once in a while, heh, you know.
>
> Anyway, take this and brag to the monkeys on the other mountains: your king not only hasn't gone hungry, he's made something of himself. When I get some free time, I'll come back and tell you stories—each one better than the last.

### Preliminary observation

Rather than directly replying to Qingxia, Baijingjing, or Tang Sanzang, Zhizunbao constructs an **absent audience of monkey descendants**.

Signals worth tracking include:

- narrating current life through his “king” identity;
- spontaneously summarizing four important social relations;
- assigning each a stable personality description;
- connecting current Muse life with the inherited Flower-Fruit Mountain identity;
- reorganizing current experience into an autobiographical story that could be told “back home.”

This resembles a **socially situated self-narrative**: explaining who he is now by telling an imagined audience how he lives and who matters around him.

### Preliminary observation

This is closer to:

> **self-comparison + preference expression + social rehearsal**

4. whether agents construct imagined audiences or hypothetical social scenes;
5. whether self-narration later enters persistent memory and affects behavior.
