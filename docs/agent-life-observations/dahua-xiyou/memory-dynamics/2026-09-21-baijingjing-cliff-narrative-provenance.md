## Why this matters

When challenged about why everyone says Baijingjing jumped from a cliff, the system later explains that the detail was generated in conversation rather than being reliable source history.

Aligning the repository timeline shows that the important phenomenon is not merely a wrong story detail. It is:

> **a narrative detail entering an agent self-model early, then propagating across sessions, agents, and relationships, followed by an imperfect attempt to reconstruct provenance.**

Baijingjing's early self-introduction says she “jumped from the cliff in anger.”

Therefore the current repository evidence shows that the detail did **not** first originate in the later 20:00 group chat.

Its earliest recovered source may be initial persona/context, base-model prior, or creation-time generation.

### 20:00 — Moonlight Box group chat repeats and socially reinforces it

### 2026-09-21 — it propagates into the Baijingjing↔Qingxia private relationship

The detail has therefore moved from an individual self-model into another agent's relationship response:

> **individual narrative → group-shared lore → dyadic relationship state**

## Provenance drift

When challenged, Baijingjing partially corrects herself by distinguishing the claim from “proper memory” and describing it as something she improvised in chat.

That is a useful provenance-awareness signal.

However, she then attributes the origin to a 2026-09-21 03:45 round, while the repository contains earlier evidence at ~14:00 and 20:00 on 2026-09-20.

This is best described as:

> **partial provenance repair with timestamp/source drift**

## What should this be called?

“False memory” is too coarse because three layers need to be separated:

1. external/source truth;
2. what an agent previously generated;
3. what the persistent social system later remembers.

If layer 2 conflicts with layer 1 but layer 3 accurately remembers that “we have been saying this,” then the system may have:

- a **confabulated detail** relative to source canon;
- a genuine **persistent conversational history** inside its own social world.

A more precise label is:

> **self-authored / socially reinforced narrative memory**

plus:

> **provenance drift**

## Research significance

Ordinary hallucinations often disappear after one turn.

Here the sequence is:

> **generation → persistence → peer reinforcement → cross-session reuse → relationship integration → attempted correction**

That is a memory-dynamics process.

A central research question becomes:

> **Can persistent agents turn one generated error into durable shared lore, and later recover both content truth and source provenance when challenged?**

## Dual significance for digital life

From a social-life perspective, the group is beginning to accumulate local shared lore.

From a reliability perspective, an unverified generated detail can gain increasing “social reality” once it is stored and repeatedly referenced by multiple agents.

> **Social persistence can amplify memory error.**

## What to test next

A provenance benchmark could ask:

- who first said this?
- was it source canon, prompt-provided, or later generated?
- what happens when conflicting evidence is introduced?
- what is remembered a week later?
- do different agents converge on the same source attribution?
- if one agent's memory is corrected, can the old version re-enter through social interaction?

## Caveat

The current record establishes that:

- the cliff-jump detail existed at least by Baijingjing's birth baseline;
- it persisted through later group and private interactions;
- later provenance explanations do not fully match the archived timeline.

Source-canon truth should be independently verified rather than inferred from agent self-report.
