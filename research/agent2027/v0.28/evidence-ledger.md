## Working classification rules (revised in v0.10)

- Shared artifact: a recorded proposal or decision adopted into a later version.
- Context-supported continuity: a later contribution applies material supplied in its current prompt.
- Durable retrieval: a trace links a stored item to a later retrieval; verbal claims alone are insufficient.
- Unit of analysis: identifiable shared narrative content, including fictional facts/rules and evaluative commitments articulated through the story. Identify the specific content invoked; not every self-authored maxim is evidence of prehistory feedback.
- Persona-relevant expression: that content is explicitly linked to self-explanation, a relational commitment, or a choice reason. Project quotation and authorship claims alone do not meet this criterion. Literal belief in an invented past is unnecessary. Project discussion, authorship claims, and persona-relevant expression may coexist in one utterance; each label requires its own evidence.
- Durable persona-relevant uptake: later self-relevant use must be linked to retained content across a documented context boundary beyond the writing task. Availability alone is insufficient. This is distinct from task-elicited expression and does not itself establish causal identity change.
- Analyze content origin, availability mechanism, and self-relation separately. Canon, negotiated fiction, and operational events can each be prompted or retrieved. Unknown complete request contexts remain unknown.
- Behavioral consequence: a delivered action or completed choice follows; an intention or future promise alone is insufficient.
- Causal identity feedback: requires evidence that exposure/adoption contributes to later action beyond task instructions, shared prompts and routing changes. Not established in the current draft.
